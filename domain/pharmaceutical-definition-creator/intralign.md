# Intralign Method — Reference Material

## Full Grammar Rules

### Subject–Predicate Structure
The pharmaceutical term is ALWAYS the grammatical subject of every sentence.
Each business rule provides the predicate (verb + object).

Correct: "Drug Substance is a material that contains Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient."
Incorrect: "A material that contains Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient is called Drug Substance."

### Simple Clauses
Use simple clauses only. Avoid compound-complex structures. Break complex
relationships into multiple simple business rules.

Correct (three separate rules):
  "Clinical Trial is a research study that evaluates investigational medicinal
  products in human subjects.
  Clinical Trial must be conducted under approved protocol.
  Clinical Trial must comply with ICH-GCP standards."

Incorrect (chained):
  "Clinical Trial is a research study that evaluates investigational medicinal
  products in human subjects and which must be conducted under approved protocol
  and which must comply with ICH-GCP standards."

### Modal Verbs — Precise Meanings
- `must` = Absolute requirement (regulatory, GxP, quality-critical, safety-critical)
- `should` = Strong recommendation or good practice guidance
- `may` = Optional or discretionary

Examples:
- "Batch Release **must** be approved by Qualified Person before distribution."
- "Process Validation **should** include at least three consecutive successful batches."
- "Stability Study **may** include accelerated, intermediate, and long-term conditions."

### Present Tense Only
Always write in present tense.
Correct: "Quality Control testing verifies conformance to specifications."
Incorrect: "Quality Control testing will verify conformance to specifications."

### Active Voice Preference
Prefer active voice. Passive is acceptable when the actor is irrelevant or follows
GxP documentation conventions.
Active: "Qualified Person approves batch release."
Passive (acceptable): "Drug Substance is manufactured under ICH Q7 GMP standards."

### Forbidden Constructions
- Never use the term being defined within its own Part 2 classification rule (circular)
- No subordinate clauses where the term appears as object
- No vague verbs: use precise pharmaceutical verbs (contains, comprises, undergoes,
  produces, evaluates, controls) not "have" or "involve"
- No ambiguous pronouns — always use the full term name
- No CRUD language (create, read, update, delete)
- No process-oriented language — use declarative statements about states and constraints

## Ambiguity Elimination Checklist

Run before finalising any Intralign definition.

- [ ] No words with multiple interpretations without qualification
  (e.g., if both "lot" and "batch" are in the glossary, distinguish them)
- [ ] All referenced pharmaceutical terms are defined elsewhere in the glossary or
  are universally understood in the subdomain
- [ ] No circular references
- [ ] Definition works independently without contextual assumptions
- [ ] Lifecycle boundaries are stated (when something becomes this term, when it ceases)
- [ ] Quantifiers are explicit: one, one or more, zero or more (not implied)
- [ ] Exclusions are stated if needed to prevent misinterpretation
- [ ] Regulatory context is clear if applicable
- [ ] Cross-functional consistency confirmed (same meaning across R&D, Manufacturing,
  Quality, Regulatory, Commercial)

## Business Rule Type Examples

### Functional Rule — what the term DOES or its PURPOSE
Pattern: active verb (provides, controls, monitors, evaluates, supports, enables)
"Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient **provides** the therapeutic or prophylactic
effect in a medicinal product."

### Consequential Rule — IMPACT or outcome
Pattern: results in, triggers, requires, leads to
"Batch failure **results in** investigation under deviation management procedures
and **may trigger** product recall if released."

### Formula Rule — CALCULATIONS or mathematical relationships
Pattern: is calculated as, is expressed as, is defined as [formula]
"Drug Substance Yield is calculated as actual output mass divided by theoretical
output mass, expressed as percentage."

### Conditional Rule — WHEN or UNDER WHAT CIRCUMSTANCES
Pattern: is required when, applies if, is triggered when
"Batch Review is required **when** critical process parameters deviate from
validated ranges."

### Characteristic Rule — inherent ATTRIBUTES or properties
Pattern: must be identified by, must include, must meet
"Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient must be identified using ISO 11238 substance
identification standards."
"Clinical Trial Protocol must include study objectives, design, methodology,
statistical considerations, and organisation."

### Compositional Rule — what the term CONTAINS or CONSISTS OF
Pattern: comprises, contains, consists of
"Drug Product **comprises** Drug Substance, Excipients, and Packaging Components."
"Batch Record **contains** Master Batch Record reference, execution documentation,
in-process control results, and batch disposition."

## Worked Example 1 — Scientific Term

**Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient** (Abbreviation: API)
Synonyms: Drug Substance Active Component
Regulatory Alignment: ISO 11238, ICH Q7

Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient is a substance that provides pharmacological
activity or therapeutic effect in a medicinal product.

Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient must be identified using ISO 11238 substance
identification standards.

Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient must be manufactured in accordance with ICH Q7
Good Manufacturing Practice guidelines.

Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient is distinguished from Excipient by its intended
pharmacological or therapeutic function.

Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient may comprise a single molecular entity or a
defined mixture of molecular entities.

Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient purity must meet pharmacopoeial specifications
or validated in-house specifications.

## Worked Example 2 — Process Term

**Process Validation** (Abbreviation: PV)
Synonyms: None
Regulatory Alignment: FDA Process Validation Guidance, EMA Annex 15

Process Validation is a documented programme that provides a high degree of
assurance that a manufacturing process consistently produces output meeting
predetermined quality attributes.

Process Validation must be conducted in accordance with regulatory guidance and
quality system requirements.

Process Validation comprises three stages: Process Design, Process Qualification,
and Continued Process Verification.

Process Qualification should include at least three consecutive successful batches
manufactured under commercial conditions.

Process Validation is required before routine commercial manufacturing commences.

Process Validation must be reviewed and updated when significant changes to
process, equipment, or materials occur.

## Worked Example 3 — Material Term

**Clinical Trial Material** (Abbreviation: CTM)
Synonyms: Investigational Medicinal Product, IMP
Regulatory Alignment: ICH-GCP, EU Clinical Trials Regulation

Clinical Trial Material is a pharmaceutical dosage form that is tested or used as
reference in a clinical trial.

Clinical Trial Material comprises investigational product, placebo, or active
comparator.

Clinical Trial Material must be manufactured in accordance with GMP Annex 13
requirements.

Clinical Trial Material must be labelled in accordance with clinical trial protocol
and regulatory requirements.

Clinical Trial Material accountability must be maintained from manufacturing through
final disposition.

Clinical Trial Material may be blinded or unblinded depending on study design.

## Worked Example 4 — Quality Term

**Out of Specification Result** (Abbreviation: OOS)
Synonyms: OOS Result
Regulatory Alignment: FDA OOS Guidance

Out of Specification Result is an analytical measurement that falls outside
established acceptance criteria for a quality attribute.

Out of Specification Result must be investigated under deviation management
procedures.

Out of Specification Result investigation must determine root cause and assess
impact on product quality.

Out of Specification Result may be classified as laboratory error, manufacturing
deviation, or specification failure.

Out of Specification Result that is confirmed valid must be evaluated for batch
disposition decision.

Out of Specification Result reporting must be completed within established
timeframes per quality system procedures.

## Worked Example 5 — Regulatory Role

**Qualified Person** (Abbreviation: QP)
Synonyms: None (EU-specific term)
Regulatory Alignment: EU Directive 2001/83/EC

Qualified Person is an individual who holds the statutory responsibility for
certifying that medicinal product batches meet regulatory requirements before
release to market.

Qualified Person must possess qualifications specified in EU pharmaceutical
legislation.

Qualified Person certifies that each batch has been manufactured and checked in
compliance with applicable regulations and marketing authorisation.

Qualified Person maintains records of batch certification in the batch disposition
record.

Qualified Person may delegate specific activities but retains ultimate
accountability for batch certification.

Qualified Person authority is recognised only within the EU regulatory framework.
